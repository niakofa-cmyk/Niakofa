I would redesign the navigation controls around the bird
Most maps have floating circular buttons.
I'd integrate them into the bird's behavior.
For example:

Recenter Map
The bird briefly rises above the map.
Everything recenters beneath it.
Then it lands back over your position.

Start Navigation
Instead of a "Start" button simply switching screens:
The bird stretches.
Spreads both wings.
Points toward the destination.
Then begins flying.

Compass Button

Tap three times:
Bird locks to North.
Three navigation modes.

One enhancement I would add that wasn't in the original concept
I would make the bird anticipate movement, not just react to it.
For example:
* If the map route shows an upcoming left turn, the bird glances left a moment before the instruction.
* As the user approaches their destination, the bird gradually slows, flaps less, and begins descending into a hover.
* When arriving, it performs a gentle landing animation instead of abruptly stopping.
These small anticipatory behaviors make the mascot feel intelligent rather than mechanically driven.
Overall assessment
I think this is an excellent direction because it transforms the Sankofa Bird from a decorative icon into a core part of Niakofa's experience. The key is to avoid making it feel like a collectible game creature. Instead, make it a trusted guide whose movement, reactions, and flight all reinforce the app's mission of guidance, care, and community. If you build it with a layered vector rig, a Rive state machine, realistic flight-inspired animation, and meaningful interactions tied to navigation and helping others, the mascot could become one of the most distinctive features of the app.
