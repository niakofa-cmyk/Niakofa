Bugs I found while verifying
1. Duration logging bug on errors. In chat.ts, streamStartTime is declared once outside the try block (let streamStartTime = 0) and then redeclared with const inside the try block. The catch block references the outer variable, which never gets updated — so every failed request logs a duration of "time since Unix epoch" instead of the actual elapsed time. Harmless to users, but it's quietly corrupting your error-latency metrics.
2. Stale year hardcoded into search queries. The continuous-learning worker's search queries are literally "Fort Worth Texas community news mutual aid neighbors helping 2025", "...2025" for all four topics. It's July 2026 now — these should build the year dynamically (new Date().getFullYear()), or Nia is quietly biasing her "current" local knowledge toward last year.
3. A real safety gap, worth prioritizing: crisis detection is English/Spanish only, but Nia's identity isn't. culturalGreetings.ts supports Swahili, Akan/Twi, Zulu, Yoruba, and Luganda — core to your vision of an assistant that centers the African diaspora. But safety.ts's crisis and soft-distress patterns only exist in English and Spanish. If someone in genuine crisis writes to Nia in Swahili or Yoruba, right now she has no way to detect it and route them to 988/211. Given your explicit "never lower the floor" comment already in that file, this is the highest-value gap to close — not a nice-to-have.
