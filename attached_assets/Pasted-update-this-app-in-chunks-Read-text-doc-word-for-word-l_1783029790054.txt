Where I'd push you to look next — these are real gaps, not nitpicks:
1. Vetting is thinner than payment. There's a background_check_status field on users, but no actual background-check provider wired in — it looks like a manual/stub status, not an automated Checkr-style integration. Identity verification (Stripe Identity, photo-ID match) is real, but that confirms who someone is, not that they're safe to let into a home for senior_care or childcare. Given those are explicit categories in your app, I'd treat this as the top priority gap before scaling.
2. No content moderation on request descriptions. Community/gratitude posts run through a moderation filter before going public — help requests themselves don't. Nothing stops someone using the "other" category to describe something illegal or unsafe in free text with no automatic screening, only after-the-fact user reports.
3. No liability/insurance or waiver flow in the code. For tasks like moving labor, home repair, or senior care, you'll want this addressed legally (ToS/waiver) even if it doesn't live in code — I didn't find any trace of it, which suggests it hasn't been addressed yet either way.
4. Pool sustainability is honestly the biggest open question. Right now funding sources for the pool seem to be requester repayments and gov sponsors — I didn't find a direct "the public can donate into the pool" endpoint. If the pool runs dry, helpers still get eventually paid via the queued backfill, but that's a soft spot worth stress-testing before you lean hard on "helpers get paid every day."
5. The guaranteed minimum is currently a flat per-task floor, not tied to time/effort. If "livable wage" is core to your pitch, you may want that floor to scale with estimated hours rather than being one flat number regardless of job size.

The gaps — and this part is worth knowing
Interestingly, the codebase's own CLAUDE.md has a section titled "Known product gaps — owner briefing," dated today (July 2, 2026), where a prior session already recorded almost exactly the tension in your vision. Paraphrasing what it says:
1. Pay-it-forward currently puts the payment risk on the helper, not the platform. As built, the helper's wallet is only credited when the requester's Stripe payment actually succeeds — meaning if a requester never pays, the helper earns nothing for that task. The note says achieving "helpers earn a livable wage" requires the pool to be funded (sponsors/county/grants) so it can front payment reliably, and a real guaranteed-minimum budget — not just the mechanism, but actual money behind it.
2. Task categories are narrow — groceries, transportation, errands, home repair, medical, emergency, other. Missing: moving/labor, pet care, tech help, tutoring, childcare, senior care, yard work, business services. No per-category liability/insurance handling (medical and home repair carry very different risk than groceries).
3. No pledge default handling — no reminder escalation, no real write-off process, no trust-score consequence for people who never pay.
4. A real legal flag, not a code flag: letting someone pay for services rendered "whenever they have the funds — in 2 days, 2 months, or 2 years" is functionally extending consumer credit. At scale that can trigger Truth-in-Lending, state usury caps, or lending-license requirements depending on your state. This is the one I'd take most seriously — it's worth a conversation with an actual lawyer before this scales past friends-and-neighbors usage in Tarrant County. I'm not able to give legal advice here, just flagging that the codebase itself is right to flag it.
5. Tax reporting: helpers paid via Stripe Connect will cross 1099-K/1099-NEC thresholds — that needs to be surfaced to helpers, not just handled silently by Stripe.
6. Business accounts are underbuilt — the flag exists, but there's no real business flow (bulk requests, invoicing, recurring service agreements) yet.
What I'd add on top of that
* Nothing currently prevents someone from spamming pay_it_forward claims and never paying anyone — there's no cap on outstanding pledge exposure per user before the platform requires them to switch to immediate.
* The guaranteed-minimum amount is a system_settings value an admin sets — right now nothing enforces it stays sustainable against pool balance in real time beyond a low-balance alert; worth a dashboard showing pool runway before you promote this feature publicly.
* Worth deciding now, before scale: is the "livable wage" framing hourly-equivalent, per-task-flat, or tiered by task difficulty/urgency? The schema doesn't encode task effort/duration anywhere yet, which you'd need for genuinely livable per-task payouts.
