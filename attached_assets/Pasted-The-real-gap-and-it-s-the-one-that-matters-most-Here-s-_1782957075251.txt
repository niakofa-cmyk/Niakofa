The real gap — and it's the one that matters most
Here's the thing the code reveals that the mission statement glosses over: when a request is pay_it_forward, the helper gets paid nothing until the requester's Stripe charge actually succeeds. I read the webhook handler directly — the helper's benevolence_wallet is only credited inside payment_intent.succeeded. There is no mechanism where a community pool or county fund pays the helper now and collects from the requester later. Right now, "pay it forward" is really "pay whenever, or maybe never" from the helper's perspective — the helper is the one carrying the risk, not a fund.
That's a direct conflict with the vision you described: "Helpers will be paid... just by waking up and helping others each and everyday... a livable wage." As built, a helper's income is entirely dependent on strangers eventually following through, with no floor. If you want helpers to actually be able to count on this as income, you need Both:

A funded pool (sponsors, county, grants, or Niakofa itself) that fronts the payment to the helper immediately, and the requester's later repayment replenishes the pool instead of paying the helper directly. And
A guaranteed minimum per completed task (e.g., a base "thank you" amount) funded by the platform/sponsors, with pay-it-forward as a bonus on top. Other gaps worth knowing about

Task scope is narrow vs. "almost any legal task." Categories are currently just groceries, transportation, errands, home_repair, medical, emergency, other. No moving/labor, pet care, tech help, tutoring, childcare, senior care, yard work, business services, etc. other exists as a catch-all but there's no real taxonomy or per-category safety/liability handling (e.g., "medical" and "home_repair" probably deserve different insurance/licensing disclaimers than "groceries").
No collections or default handling for pledges. If someone never pays their pay-it-forward pledge, nothing happens — no reminder escalation beyond a worker (pledge-worker.ts) I saw referenced, no write-off process, no impact on trust score.
Worth a flag, not legal advice: letting people defer payment for services rendered over months or years is functionally extending credit. Depending on how it's structured and at what scale, that can brush up against consumer-lending rules (Truth in Lending, state usury/licensing laws) in ways a simple "pay whenever" feature might not anticipate. Worth a conversation with an actual lawyer before this goes live at scale — I'm not one.
Tax reporting for helpers isn't visible in what I checked — once helpers are routinely being paid through Stripe Connect for tasks, 1099-K/1099-NEC reporting obligations kick in at certain thresholds. Worth confirming Stripe's handling this and that it's surfaced to helpers.
Business-side request flow: account types support "business," but I didn't see anything distinct about how a business posts/pays for help vs. an individual (bulk requests, invoicing, recurring service agreements) — if businesses are a real audience for you, that's currently underbuilt.
