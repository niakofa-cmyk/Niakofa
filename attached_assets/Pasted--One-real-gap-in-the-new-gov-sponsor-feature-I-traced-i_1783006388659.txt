
One real gap in the new gov-sponsor feature
I traced it end to end: gov-sponsors.ts handles application and admin approval only. Once a county is approved, there's no endpoint or UI connecting that approval to an actual pool contribution — no sponsor_contribution record tied to a government_sponsor_id, no "fund the pool" action for an approved county to take. Right now it's a vetted directory of eligible government partners, not yet a funding pipe. That's the natural next step if you want a county to actually be able to put money into the pool through the portal, rather than just being on an approved list.

Two things remain genuinely open, unchanged from before:
1. The "livable wage" mechanic — still no per-hour floor or volume bonus, just a flat guaranteed minimum per task.
2. Regulatory groundwork (money-transmitter licensing, 1099 reporting) — not a code task, worth a lawyer's input before real money moves at scale, especially now that government entities may be involved. spec out the gov-sponsor → pool funding endpoint next

The new thing: Government/County Sponsors — this is real movement toward your "county portal"
Migration 0029 adds a government_sponsors table + /gov-sponsors routes, following the same approval-queue pattern as businesses. A county or government entity can apply as a named community pool sponsor — same idea as pay-it-forward, but the funding side, not the request side. It's not a literal integration with county government systems (no data feed, no county caseworker login), but it's a legitimate first step: a real mechanism for a county to formally back the pool that fronts helper payments, with admin review before anything goes live. Given your original vision language — "through a county's open portal or connection" — I'd read this as the platform reaching toward the funding-partner interpretation of that phrase rather than a literal government API integration. Worth deciding which one you actually meant, since they're very different builds from here.
Small things worth a look, not urgent
* The spending-cap query sums a column literally named pay_it_forward_amount even for immediate payments — that field's just being reused as a generic "offer amount," which is legacy naming debt from before business accounts existed, not a new bug. Still, it's the kind of thing that trips up the next person reading this route cold. A rename to something like offer_amount would pay for itself.
* Also snuck into this diff: several unrelated race-condition fixes on request status transitions (en-route/arrived updates now check the current status atomically instead of trusting the caller's prior read, returning 409 instead of a misleading 404 on conflict) and a NaN-guard on helper_id/requester_id query params that would previously have produced a malformed SQL query. Good defensive hardening, unprompted.
