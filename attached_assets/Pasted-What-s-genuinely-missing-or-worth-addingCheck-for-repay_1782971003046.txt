1. No repayment reminder/nudge system. A requester who picks Pay It Forward with no deadline has zero built-in nudge to ever repay — no "hey, whenever you're able" email/push at 2 days, 2 weeks, 2 months. Without this, the pool only gets replenished by new sponsors, not by past requesters, and it'll drain over time.
2. Pool sustainability isn't modeled. There's no dashboard or logic tracking whether inflow (sponsors + repayments) is keeping pace with outflow (fronted payments + guaranteed minimums). Right now it's a ledger, not a forecast — worth adding a simple "runway" metric so you know if the pool is heading toward zero.
3. "Livable wage" isn't actually guaranteed anywhere in the code — only a flat guaranteed minimum per task. If the vision is "helpers get paid a livable wage just for waking up and helping," that implies something like a per-hour floor or volume-based bonus, which doesn't exist yet.
4. County/government integration is a business/legal project, not a code gap. The pool architecture supports a county as a sponsor, but actually plugging into a county's real portal means partnership agreements, possibly public funds compliance — that's outside what any codebase can solve.

Regulatory exposure worth flagging (not a coding task, but real): fronting payments and holding a pool of funds that gets repaid later can brush up against money-transmitter licensing depending on your state, and 1099 reporting for helpers who are effectively earning income. Worth a conversation with a lawyer before scaling this, not something I can advise on.
6. No collections-free write-off path. If someone never repays, does the debt just live forever in the ledger? A "forgiven" or "written off" entry type would keep the ledger honest and let the pool stop chasing pennies.
